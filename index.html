The four priority colors

What each color means

Every patient is assessed on arrival and assigned one of four colors based on symptoms, vital signs, and risk — not on how long they've been waiting.

Red · Emergency

Immediate

Seen right away
  • Difficulty breathing or unconscious
  • Severe bleeding or chest pain
  • Signs of stroke or shock
Orange · Urgent

Within 10 min

Rechecked while waiting
  • High fever with worsening symptoms
  • Deep wounds needing sutures
  • Moderate difficulty breathing
Yellow · Priority

Within 60 min

Stable, still needs prompt care
  • Persistent vomiting or dehydration
  • Sprains, minor fractures
  • Uncontrolled chronic condition
Green · Non-urgent

As slots open

Stable, routine care
  • Check-ups and follow-ups
  • Prescription renewals
  • Mild colds, minor complaints
Start to finish

The triage process, step by step

The same five steps apply to every patient, whether it's a routine visit or an emergency — only the pace changes.

01

Arrival & registration

Give your name and reason for the visit at the front desk. Bring a valid ID and, if you have one, your RHU patient record or barangay health card.

Front desk~2 min
02

Initial assessment

A triage nurse checks your vital signs — temperature, blood pressure, pulse, oxygen level — and asks about your symptoms and how long you've had them.

Triage nurse~5 min
03

Color assignment

Based on that assessment, you're assigned Red, Orange, Yellow, or Green and given a queue number in that lane. This decides your place in line, not your arrival time.

AutomaticInstant
04

Waiting area

Wait in the area matching your color lane. Yellow and Green patients may be reassessed and re-prioritized if their condition changes while waiting.

MonitoredVaries
05

Consultation

A doctor or medical officer sees you when your number is called, reviews your triage notes, and proceeds with diagnosis, treatment, or referral.

Consultation room10–20 min
Before you arrive

Make triage faster for everyone

Bring with you

  • One valid government or barangay ID
  • RHU patient record book, if issued
  • List of current medications and dosages
  • Previous lab results or referral letters, if any
  • PhilHealth ID, if applying it to your visit

Good to know

  • Triage color can change if your condition changes while waiting — tell staff right away if you feel worse
  • A companion may stay with you, but keep the waiting area clear for those seeking care
  • Walk-ins are welcome; scheduled follow-ups are still triaged on arrival
  • For a true emergency, call ahead if possible so the triage bay can prepare

Questions

Frequently asked

Why is someone who arrived after me being seen first?

Triage orders patients by medical urgency, not arrival time. A Red or Orange patient will always be seen before a Yellow or Green patient, even if they arrived later.

Can my triage color change while I wait?

Yes. If your symptoms worsen — or ease — while you wait, tell a staff member so you can be reassessed and reassigned if needed.

Do I need an appointment?

No appointment is required for triage. Walk-ins are assessed in the order they check in, then queued by urgency.

What if my case isn't an emergency?

Non-urgent cases are still seen the same day whenever possible, in the Green lane, once Red, Orange, and Yellow patients are addressed.
